@AraafRoyall That is rotten and I am sorry it happened.

First, recovery: if you ever exported any of the three as .asc files, or made a backup from Settings, importing those brings them back completely. If you uploaded the public keys to a keyserver, the public halves can be fetched again, which at least lets people keep encrypting to you if you still have the private material somewhere. If none of those exist, the private keys are gone; there is no hidden copy anywhere, by design.

On the feature: I cannot remove it, because a working reset matters for people handing a device on or starting over. But you are right that it fired too easily, and the next build gates it properly: several consecutive confirmation steps, each with its own acknowledgement to tick, plus a biometric check before anything is wiped. One dialog and one checkbox was not enough friction for something this destructive.

If you can tell me whether you had any exports or backups, I can walk you through getting back to where you were.

@CertainBot fair observation, and here is the threat model as I see it: that sheet only appears after the export warning and, when the device supports it, the biometric check. Anyone who can read the "already has a passphrase" note has already authenticated far enough to export the key material itself and check in two seconds, so the note reveals nothing an attacker could not get more directly. Hiding it would cost honest users the answer to "is this export protected or not" at exactly the moment they need it. If you can sketch an attacker who benefits from the note but could not just export the key, I will reconsider.
