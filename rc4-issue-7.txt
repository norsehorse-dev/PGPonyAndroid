Thank you, and thanks for the detail. You have found two separate bugs, and the OpenKeychain comparison is what makes them easy to tell apart.

**The crash is fixed in 4.0.4, released tonight.**

Release builds were renaming part of the OpenPGP API contract that PGPony and the mail client both depend on. The client then could not resolve the object PGPony sent back after a decrypt, and threw. This affected Thunderbird, K-9 and FairEmail equally, because they all use the same client library and the same call. Debug builds were unaffected, which is exactly why my own provider testing never caught it.

OpenKeychain works because its release build is not mangling that shared contract. Nothing clever on their side, a plain bug on mine.

4.0.4 is on GitHub and pgpony.app now. F-Droid and Play will follow on their own schedules, so if you installed from one of those it will take a little longer to reach you. Worth telling me which one you used, and your current version, in case anything still goes wrong after updating.

**The Yubico Authenticator launching on its own is a separate issue, and it is known.**

You are the second person to report it. The cause is that PGPony is not holding exclusive control of the NFC reader while a card operation runs. When your YubiKey is still in the field afterwards, Android's own tag dispatch reads the tag and hands it to whichever app claims that record, which is Yubico Authenticator. The fix is to hold NFC reader mode for the whole session with the platform's NDEF handling suppressed, which is what OpenKeychain does. It is targeted for the next release rather than this one.

It would help me to know one thing once you are on 4.0.4: does the Yubico Authenticator still open by itself now that the crash is gone? A crash can drop the NFC session on its own, so with the crash removed I can tell whether the two were connected in your case or merely happened together. Either answer is useful.

@christian179717 

I'm assuming you alread have PGPony installed and configured with your key.

Go to: Thunderbird > Settings > Accounts > "yourmail@provider.com" > End-to-end encryption

Disable the first switch, and enable it again, it will prompt you to select which app to use: OpenKeychain or PGPony (I have both installed)
Then click on "Configure end-to-end key" , and select your key.
That's it! Now your PGPony should be able to encrypt/decrypt your e-mails on Thunderbird.
