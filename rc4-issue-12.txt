You have found it, and I owe you an apology: I did not understand this report the first two times and I went looking in the wrong place twice. The screenshots with the file sizes on them made it obvious. Thank you for the persistence.

The number is 4 MB, not 1 MB. Above that, file decrypt takes a different code path that streams to disk instead of holding the file in memory, and that path never even attempts to read the bundle structure. It is hardcoded to show a single file. So it is not that large bundles fail sometimes; every bundle over 4 MB shows one file, one hundred percent of the time, and every bundle under it works. Your 1.1 MB ar.asc worked for that reason and your 11.7 MB message.asc could not have.

"Maybe up to 4 files inside" is total size, not file count. Four small photos stay under 4 MB. Five 2 MB photos do not. That is why it looked like a count.

The single file is not corrupted. It is the bundle container itself, written out whole with no extension, so nothing can open it. Your files are inside it and intact. That is no comfort when you cannot get at them, but it does mean nothing was lost.

Also noted and confirmed:

No progress bar on bundle encrypt. Correct, the single-file path got one and bundle never did.
Re-picking duplicates the list. Correct, and it is a small fix. The picker does not remember your earlier selection, so reselecting adds everything again.
Attachments list position. 4.1.0 collapses the list past 8 files so the buttons stay reachable. Tell me which version you are on and I will see whether that is enough or whether it should move as well.

One thing I want to check before I start, and it worries me more than the rest. Your encrypt screenshot has "Also sign this file" switched on, and the decrypt screenshot says "No signature". If those are the same bundle, then PGPony told you it signed something and told the recipient it did not, which would be a much more serious problem than the attachment list. Were those two screenshots the same file, or different tests?

This is my number one priority now. I will also run your 10 files at 2 MB each, because I suspect the encrypt side has a memory ceiling of its own that nobody has hit yet.

Your suggestion about extracting everything and listing it on its own page is close to what the fix will have to be. Holding a dozen decrypted photos in memory is exactly what the 4 MB limit exists to avoid, so writing them out and listing them from disk solves both problems at once.

.asc alongside it, same signing key as rc2, installs over the top.

Everything above is in it. Chasing your report also turned up a run of things nobody had reported, so those are in too:

Signature reporting was wrong in two ways. A message signed by someone whose key you do not hold was reported as "not signed", which is false — it was signed, the app just could not say by whom. You now get a yellow "unknown signer" banner you can tap to go and fetch their key. And the bundle result screen never showed signature information at all, for any bundle, signed or not. Every other decrypt screen did. Both fixed.

Bundle signing had no key picker. Every other mode has had "Sign as" since 3.x; bundles never got it, so with more than one keypair you could turn signing on and had no say in which key signed.

Worse, and this is the one that bothered me: if your default signer was a hardware key, the sign section disappeared from bundle compose entirely, so you could not sign a bundle at all. And if the toggle happened to already be on, it quietly produced an unsigned bundle while the screen said it was signing. Sender told signed, recipient told unsigned. That is precisely the thing I was worried your screenshots showed, and it turned out to be reachable by a completely different route. It now refuses with a visible error instead.

Hardware keys can sign bundles now, which was never a technical limitation, just something scoped out of the original bundle work. Large hardware-key-encrypted bundles open properly too — before this you could sign one and then not be able to read it.

Most of that traces back to you pushing on this three times. The 10-files test in particular was a genuinely separate failure I would not have gone looking for.
