You're right, and this is the shape I want PGPony to land on for interoperable PQC encryption.

RFC 9980 §3.5 carves out exactly one exception to the v6 rule: ML-KEM-768+X25519 (algorithm 35) is allowed in v4 encryption-capable subkeys, and §4.3.2 makes it normative (algo 35 with v4 or v6, algo 36 / ML-KEM-1024+X448 v6-only). A v4 Ed25519 primary with a v4 algo-35 subkey means the primary is an ordinary v4 key every tool already parses, and only the encryption subkey needs algo-35 support. That is a much lower bar than asking a correspondent's software to handle a full v6 certificate, and it matches how uneven v6 support still is in end-user clients.

The one limit worth stating up front: the v4 allowance is 768 only. ML-KEM-1024 (algo 36) stays v6-only, so a v4 interop key is an ML-KEM-768 key. That is fine for the interop target; 768 is the level most tools implement first anyway.

The plan is to add this as a keygen option and make it the recommended default for people who want PQC encryption that actually reaches their correspondents, alongside the existing v6 shapes for anyone who wants 1024 or a full post-quantum certificate. The Appendix A.2 sample key and vectors give a concrete conformance target, the same way the v6 vectors did for the earlier work.

On v5: agreed, and I will fix the framing. The LibrePGP algo-8 / v5 composites are effectively GnuPG-only and are not going to be a broad interop story. PGPony will keep them for GnuPG interop but stop presenting them as a generally interoperable option, and steer the UX toward the IETF v4/v6 path instead.

Thanks for pointing at the exact RFC section and the sample key. That is the detail that makes this straightforward to build against.

This shipped in 4.5.0-RC2. PGPony now generates the v4 ML-KEM-768 + X25519 shape (algorithm 35): a v4 Ed25519 primary with the algo-35 encryption subkey, so the primary is an ordinary v4 key any tool already parses and only the subkey needs algo-35 support. It is in the keygen picker as the recommended post-quantum interop option, alongside the v6 shapes for anyone who wants 1024 or a full v6 certificate. Encrypt and decrypt round-trip against the RFC 9980 Appendix A.2 sample, in both the v3-PKESK/SEIPDv1 and v6-PKESK/SEIPDv2 framings.

I also reworded the v5 framing the way we discussed. The LibrePGP algo-8 composites are now presented as GnuPG interop only rather than a broad interop story, and the UX steers toward the IETF v4/v6 path.
